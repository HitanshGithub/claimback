# CASE-006: Room rent cap - Partly allowed (State Commission West Bengal, 2018)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** State Commission West Bengal (West Bengal State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, Kolkata)
- **Case reference:** First Appeal No. A/98/2017 (arising from CC/508/2013, District Forum Kolkata-I (North)) - Niraj Kajaria v. United India Insurance Co. Ltd.
- **Decision date:** 2018-08-09
- **Category:** room_rent_cap (Room rent cap)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - United India Insurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** United India mediclaim policy (sum insured Rs 4,25,000)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 2,41,204
- **Amount in dispute:** not stated
- **Outcome:** Partly allowed (policyholder partly won)
- **Amount awarded:** not stated

## Facts
The complainant claimed Rs 2,41,204 for his wife's hospitalisation in September 2012: a hospital bill of Rs 95,842 plus Rs 1,45,362 towards doctor fees paid separately by cheque, implant, medicines and pathology. The insurer paid Rs 1,29,056. The wife stayed five days in rooms charged at Rs 9,000 and Rs 5,400 per day against an entitlement of Rs 4,250 per day. The District Forum dismissed the complaint.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
The patient occupied rooms above her entitlement, so room rent was capped and doctor, nursing and other expenses were reimbursed proportionately; doctor fees paid directly outside the hospital bill were not reimbursed.

## Decision
Partly allowed (policyholder partly won). No specific amount quantified in the decision (or no amount awarded).

## Reasoning
The Commission upheld the room-rent deduction of Rs 12,950 as fully justified by the 1%-of-sum-insured cap (Rs 4,250 x 5 days). But it found the proportionate deductions were wrongly computed - for example, on a day when the room was 21.29% above eligibility, 52.77% of the OT equipment charge was deducted - and held that doctor fees paid directly could not be refused without written reasons, given the insurer's own head-office guideline. The insurer was ordered to recompute, pay the balance with 9% interest, furnish a calculation sheet and pay Rs 25,000 litigation costs.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- Room rent entitlement 1% of sum insured per day
- Proportionate reimbursement of other expenses for higher room category
- Insurer's head-office guideline on reimbursement of doctor fees paid by cheque

## Key quote
> "the Respondents did not act bona fide while figuring out the admissible amount"
