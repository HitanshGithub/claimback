# CASE-009: Non-payable items / consumables - Dismissed (State Commission Delhi, 2021)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** State Commission Delhi (Delhi State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission)
- **Case reference:** First Appeal No. 1270/2013 (arising from Complaint No. 1249 of 2010, District Forum, I.P. Estate, New Delhi) - National Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Amit Mittal
- **Decision date:** 2021-02-25
- **Category:** non_payable_consumables (Non-payable items / consumables)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - National Insurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** National Insurance mediclaim policy (2009-10)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 88,067
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 78,490
- **Outcome:** Dismissed (insurer's decision upheld)
- **Amount awarded:** Rs 9,527

## Facts
The complainant was hospitalised in Gurgaon in November 2009 after a fall injured his left knee. He claimed Rs 88,067, which included a knee support, equipment charges, small items such as micropore and bandages, and physiotherapy/doctor visits at home, but the TPA approved only Rs 9,527. The District Forum awarded the full claim with 9% interest and Rs 35,000, and the insurer appealed.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Deductions under exclusion 4.16 (external/durable medical equipment usable at home, e.g., knee support), 4.17 (non-medical items) and 4.26 (doctor's home visits and nursing/physiotherapy at home during pre- and post-hospitalisation), totalling Rs 78,490.

## Decision
Dismissed (insurer's decision upheld). Amount awarded: Rs 9,527.

## Reasoning
Applying the principle that insurance contracts are construed strictly (Sushilaben Indravadan Gandhi v. New India Assurance, SC 2020), the State Commission held the deducted items fell within the exclusion clauses. It set aside the District Forum's order and directed payment of only the originally approved Rs 9,527 (with 9% interest if not paid by 31.03.2021).

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- Exclusion 4.16 - external/durable medical and non-medical equipment
- Exclusion 4.17 - non-medical expenses
- Exclusion 4.26 - doctor's home visit / attendant / nursing during pre- and post-hospitalisation
- Sushilaben Indravadan Gandhi v. New India Assurance (2020 SCC OnLine SC 367)

## Key quote
> "the Insurance Company is permitted to make deduction of the aforesaid expenses from the insurance claim as per the exclusionary clauses in the insurance policy"
