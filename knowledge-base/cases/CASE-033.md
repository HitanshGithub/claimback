# CASE-033: Modern treatment methods - Allowed (State Commission Gujarat, 2023)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** State Commission Gujarat (Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, Gujarat State, Ahmedabad)
- **Case reference:** Appeal No. 356/2023 (against District Commission Ahmedabad City (Main), Complaint No. 630/2020) - The New India Assurance Co. Ltd. v. Rajesh Dilsukhrai Dave
- **Decision date:** 2023-04-29
- **Category:** modern_treatment (Modern treatment methods)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - The New India Assurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** New India mediclaim policy (sum insured Rs 5,00,000)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 4,00,474
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 3,09,327
- **Outcome:** Allowed (policyholder won)
- **Amount awarded:** Rs 3,09,327

## Facts
The complainant underwent robotic nephrectomy for a large left kidney lesion at an Ahmedabad hospital in September 2019, incurring Rs 4,00,474. The insurer authorised only Rs 86,000 cashless (plus pre- and post-hospitalisation expenses) and deducted Rs 3,09,327, treating the robotic component as non-payable under the PPN package agreed between its TPA and the hospital; the complainant had paid the balance to the hospital. The District Commission ordered payment of Rs 3,09,327 with 8% interest, Rs 10,000 for mental agony and Rs 3,000 costs, and the insurer appealed.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Claim limited to the PPN package rate (Rs 86,000 for nephrectomy) agreed between the TPA and the network hospital; robotic charges beyond the package not payable and the hospital charged above the agreed package.

## Decision
Allowed (policyholder won). Amount awarded: Rs 3,09,327.

## Reasoning
The State Commission held that the insurance contract is between the insured and the insurer, so a package agreement between the TPA and a hospital does not bind the insured. It noted that the IRDAI modern-treatment limit for robotic surgery quoted by the insurer applied only from 01.10.2020, after this surgery, and that under the IRDAI (TPA - Health Services) Regulations, 2016 a TPA cannot itself reject claims. The insurer's appeal was dismissed at the admission stage and the District Commission order confirmed.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- IRDAI guidelines on modern treatment - robotic surgery limit (effective 01.10.2020, held inapplicable)
- IRDAI (Third Party Administrators - Health Services) Regulations, 2016, Regulation 3(2)
- New India Assurance v. Vikram Gohel (Punjab State Commission) - TPA cannot repudiate

## Key quote
> "any agreement between TPA and the treating hospital are not binding either to the Insurance Company or to the insured"
