# CASE-035: Delayed intimation / late claim submission - Allowed (State Commission Tamil Nadu, 2023)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** State Commission Tamil Nadu (Tamil Nadu State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, Chennai)
- **Case reference:** F.A. No. 206/2019 (against CC No. 190/2016, District Commission Coimbatore) - National Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Shravan Bohra
- **Decision date:** 2023-02-14
- **Category:** delayed_intimation (Delayed intimation / late claim submission)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - National Insurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** National Insurance mediclaim policy (November 2009 - November 2010)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 2,80,460
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 2,80,460
- **Outcome:** Allowed (policyholder won)
- **Amount awarded:** Rs 2,80,460

## Facts
The complainant's wife was hospitalised repeatedly in Coimbatore between April and July 2010 and died in hospital in July 2010. After performing her last rites in his native state and staying there for several months, the complainant submitted his claim for Rs 2,80,460 in January 2011, about 9 months late. The insurer rejected it as time-barred, and rejected it again in 2015 even after the State Commission had directed it to reconsider the claim as a special case under the waiver clause.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Claim not filed within 30 days of discharge as required by policy conditions 5.1, 5.3 and 5.4; delay of more than 9 months.

## Decision
Allowed (policyholder won). Amount awarded: Rs 2,80,460.

## Reasoning
Condition 5.4 itself allowed waiver of the time limit in extreme hardship, and the insurer did not dispute the treatment, the bills or the death - late submission was the only ground. Given the bereavement and the earlier direction to treat the claim as a special case, repudiation was unjustified. The Commission upheld payment of Rs 2,80,460 but reduced interest to 6% from the date of complaint and set aside compensation because the delay was admitted (costs of Rs 5,000 retained).

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- Policy condition 5.4 - claim within 30 days of discharge, with waiver in cases of extreme hardship

## Key quote
> "especially when there is no denial of the claim for anyother reason except for the delayed submission"
