# CASE-019: Pre-existing disease / non-disclosure - Allowed (NCDRC, 2024)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** NCDRC (National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission)
- **Case reference:** Revision Petition No. 563 of 2022 (against State Commission Chandigarh FA No. 49/2021) - Care Health Insurance Ltd. v. Harjinder Singh Sohal
- **Decision date:** 2024-04-10
- **Category:** ped_non_disclosure (Pre-existing disease / non-disclosure)
- **Insurer type:** standalone health insurer - Care Health Insurance Ltd. (formerly Religare Health Insurance)
- **Policy:** International / overseas travel medical insurance policy (Sept 2018 - Mar 2019)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 17,26,546
- **Amount in dispute:** not stated
- **Outcome:** Allowed (policyholder won)
- **Amount awarded:** not stated

## Facts
The complainant, aged over 60, bought an overseas medical insurance policy for September 2018 to March 2019 and, while in Australia, underwent coronary stenting in September and again in November 2018. Cashless and later reimbursement of about AUD 31,499 were refused for non-disclosure of coronary artery disease and dyslipidaemia, based on a 2014 hospital OPD card. In the proposal he had answered 'yes' to having a pre-existing disease and disclosed high blood pressure for 5 years, but had not ticked any of the specific listed diseases. The District Commission dismissed the complaint; the State Commission allowed it.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Suppression of pre-existing coronary artery disease and dyslipidaemia in breach of utmost good faith (Clause 7.1 - disclosure of information norm); the proposer was responsible for filling all proposal form columns correctly.

## Decision
Allowed (policyholder won). No specific amount quantified in the decision (or no amount awarded).

## Reasoning
The NCDRC noted that this was not a case of a false 'no' - the complainant declared that he had a pre-existing disease and disclosed hypertension - and the insurer should have asked him to complete the blank columns or arranged a medical examination for a 60+ proposer before issuing the policy. Following Manmohan Nanda v. United India Assurance (SC), an insurer that accepts premium and issues a policy despite blank columns cannot later repudiate for non-disclosure; the insurer also had no evidence of CAD treatment beyond a single 2014 visit. The State Commission's award of the claim with interest, Rs 50,000 compensation and Rs 25,000 costs was upheld.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- Manmohan Nanda v. United India Assurance Co. Ltd. (2022) 4 SCC 582, paras 55.4-55.6
- Satwant Kaur Sandhu v. New India Assurance (2009) 8 SCC 316 (relied on by insurer)
- Policy Clause 7.1 - disclosure of information norm

## Key quote
> "By accepting the premium and issuing the policy despite the blank columns, the OPs cannot later repudiate the claim based on alleged suppression or non-disclosure by the complainant."
