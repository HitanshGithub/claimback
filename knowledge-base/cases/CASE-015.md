# CASE-015: Sub-limit / capping - Allowed (Supreme Court of India, 2021)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** Supreme Court of India (civil appeal from NCDRC)
- **Case reference:** Civil Appeal No. 6778 of 2013 - Jacob Punnen & Anr. v. United India Insurance Co. Ltd.
- **Decision date:** 2021-12-09
- **Category:** sub_limit (Sub-limit / capping)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - United India Insurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** United India mediclaim policy renewed annually since 1982 (cover Rs 8,00,000 for two insureds in 2008-09)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 3,82,705
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 2,07,705
- **Outcome:** Allowed (policyholder won)
- **Amount awarded:** Rs 1,75,000

## Facts
The appellants had held a mediclaim policy with the insurer since 1982 and renewed it for March 2008-March 2009 on the insurer's renewal reminder. In June 2008 one of them underwent angioplasty and claimed Rs 3,82,705, but the insurer paid Rs 2,00,000, relying on a new term in the renewed policy capping such procedures at 70% of the policy limit subject to Rs 2,00,000 per surgery. The District Forum ordered a further Rs 1,75,000, but the State Commission and NCDRC held that the renewed policy was a fresh contract binding on the insured.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Policy terms had changed on renewal; the 2008-09 policy capped procedures such as angioplasty at 70% of the policy limit subject to an overall limit of Rs 2,00,000 per surgery, and the insured had accepted the policy document.

## Decision
Allowed (policyholder won). Amount awarded: Rs 1,75,000.

## Reasoning
The Supreme Court held that an insurer is under a duty to inform existing policyholders, at renewal, of new limitations it is imposing so that they can choose suitable cover - an obligation now also reflected in the IRDA (Health Insurance) Regulations, 2016. Accepting the renewal premium while keeping the insured unaware of the new cap was deficiency in service. The NCDRC and State Commission orders were set aside, the District Forum's award (Rs 1,75,000 plus Rs 5,000 compensation) was restored and the insurer was made to pay additional costs of Rs 50,000.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- Consumer Protection Act, 1986 - deficiency in service
- IRDA (Health Insurance) Regulations, 2016 - disclosure of changes on renewal (as discussed by the Court)
- Biman Krishna Bose v. United India Insurance (2001) 6 SCC 477

## Key quote
> "Its failure to inform the policy holders resulted in deficiency of service."
