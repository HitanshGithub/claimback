# CASE-003: Proportionate deduction - Partly allowed (State Commission Karnataka, 2022)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** State Commission Karnataka (Karnataka State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, Bengaluru)
- **Case reference:** Appeal No. 1411/2019 (arising from Complaint No. 51/2018, District Forum Chikmagalur) - V. Rama Rao v. New India Assurance
- **Decision date:** 2022-07-06
- **Category:** proportionate_deduction (Proportionate deduction)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - The New India Assurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** Mediclaim policy held since 1978, renewed as New India Mediclaim 2012 policy (sum insured Rs 3,00,000)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 3,58,384
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 2,34,413
- **Outcome:** Partly allowed (policyholder partly won)
- **Amount awarded:** Rs 1,76,029

## Facts
The complainant, aged about 80, had held a mediclaim policy with the insurer since 1978, later renewed on Mediclaim 2012 terms. In June 2017 he underwent prostate and hernia surgery at a Bengaluru hospital costing Rs 3,58,384, but the TPA paid only Rs 1,23,971. The deductions included non-payable medicines and consumables, a pro-rata (proportionate) deduction of Rs 1,14,761, the hospital discount and tax, and the Mediclaim 2012 clause paying only 80% of admissible claims for insureds over 55 without 48 months of claim-free cover. The District Forum dismissed his complaint, holding he had admitted reading the new policy terms.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Claim settled as per Mediclaim 2012 terms: room rent restricted to 1% of sum insured per day with proportionate deduction of other charges, consumables non-payable, and only 80% of the admissible claim payable for an insured over 55 years without 48 months of claim-free continuous cover.

## Decision
Partly allowed (policyholder partly won). Amount awarded: Rs 1,76,029.

## Reasoning
Relying on the Supreme Court's decision in Jacob Punnen v. United India Insurance (2021), the Commission held that the insurer had not notified this long-standing policyholder of the revised, more restrictive terms before renewal, so settling the claim on those terms was deficient. It also relied on the NCDRC ruling in New India Assurance v. A.K. Kariappa that applying a mechanical proportionate deduction, instead of finding out what the hospital would have charged for the eligible room category, is a faulty procedure. The insurer was directed to pay up to the Rs 3,00,000 sum insured less the amount paid (Rs 1,76,029) with 6% interest, Rs 25,000 compensation and Rs 10,000 costs.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- Mediclaim 2012 clause 3.2 - 80% of admissible claim for insured over 55 without 48 months claim-free cover
- Room rent limit 1% of sum insured per day with proportionate deduction
- Jacob Punnen v. United India Insurance Co. Ltd. (Supreme Court, 09.12.2021)
- New India Assurance Co. Ltd. v. A.K. Kariappa (NCDRC, Revision Petition No. 766/2011)

## Key quote
> "The procedure adopted by the insurance company was faulty."

## Notes
Quoted by the State Commission from the NCDRC order in New India Assurance v. A.K. Kariappa.
