# CASE-036: Delayed intimation / late claim submission - Dismissed (State Commission Delhi, 2026)

- **Forum:** State Commission Delhi (Delhi State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission)
- **Case reference:** First Appeal No. 151/2025 - Star Health & Allied Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Om Dev
- **Decision date:** 2026-04-30
- **Category:** delayed_intimation (Delayed intimation / late claim submission)
- **Insurer type:** standalone health insurer - Star Health and Allied Insurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** Young Star Insurance Policy (floater), sum insured Rs 5,00,000
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 48,089
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 48,089
- **Outcome:** Dismissed (insurer's decision upheld)
- **Amount awarded:** not stated

## Facts
The complainant bought an online floater policy in December 2021 and was hospitalised in a private Delhi hospital from 23 to 25 April 2022 for enteric fever, paying a bill of Rs 48,089. He informed the insurer on 30 April 2022, about seven days after admission. The claim was rejected; the District Commission allowed it with Rs 50,000 compensation, and the insurer appealed.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Intimation was not given within 24 hours of hospitalisation (claim notification condition 2D), depriving the insurer of the chance to verify; the indoor case records were in stereotyped handwriting with tampering and discrepancies, and investigation indicated a fraudulent claim (Condition 1 - misrepresentation).

## Decision
Dismissed (insurer's decision upheld). No specific amount quantified in the decision (or no amount awarded).

## Reasoning
The State Commission held that the insured deliberately delayed intimation in breach of the 24-hour notification condition, which must be strictly construed. It also relied on the investigator's report (address untraceable, a pattern of similar claims filed through the same representative, who was also an agent, and the same doctor across hospitals), found the patient's vitals normal so that hospitalisation was unwarranted, and concluded the claim was a design to defraud. The District Commission's order was set aside. Note: late intimation was combined with strong fraud findings.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- Standard Condition 2D - notification of claim within 24 hours
- Condition 1 - misrepresentation/non-disclosure
- Industrial Promotion & Investment Corporation of Orissa v. New India Assurance - strict construction

## Key quote
> "the Respondent cannot be allowed to seek relief for its own wrong"
