# CASE-038: Claim documents - Partly allowed (Insurance Ombudsman, Mumbai, 2021)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** Insurance Ombudsman, Mumbai
- **Case reference:** Complaint Ref MUM-H-049-2021-1737 (Award No. IO/MUM/A/HI/ /2021-2022)
- **Decision date:** 2021-04-15
- **Category:** documents (Claim documents)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - The New India Assurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** New India New Mediclaim 2012 Policy (sum insured Rs 5,00,000)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 3,99,557
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 40,158
- **Outcome:** Partly allowed (policyholder partly won)
- **Amount awarded:** Rs 34,577

## Facts
The complainant, insured since 1996, had coronary angioplasty in Mumbai in September 2017. Of total claims of Rs 3,99,557 (cashless plus pre- and post-hospitalisation), Rs 40,158 remained unpaid. Deductions were made for consultation, investigation and CT scan charges for want of reports, physiotherapy bills without dates, a medicine bill without prescription, stent price above the government cap, excess room rent, dietician charges and non-medical items; the complainant said all documents had already been given to the TPA.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Supporting documents not submitted (doctor's notes, 2D echo/ECG/stress test and CT scan reports, prescription); physiotherapy bills without dates and break-up; coronary stent price capped by government notification; room rent restriction; dietician and non-medical charges not payable.

## Decision
Partly allowed (policyholder partly won). Amount awarded: Rs 34,577.

## Reasoning
The Forum asked the complainant to resubmit the CT scan and other reports and prescriptions so those expenses could be paid, allowed the physiotherapy prescribed by the cardiologist, and held the excess stent charge must be reimbursed because the patient has no control over hospital billing (the insurer may recover any overcharge from the hospital). Deductions for excess room rent, dietician charges and non-medical items were upheld, and Rs 34,577 was awarded.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- Claim documentation requirements (reports, prescriptions)
- Government price cap on coronary stents
- Room rent restriction and non-medical items

## Key quote
> "the patient has no control on the same and has genuinely paid the amount as billed by the hospital"
