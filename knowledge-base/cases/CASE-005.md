# CASE-005: Proportionate deduction - Dismissed (State Commission West Bengal, 2019)

- **Case reference:** First Appeal No. A/465/2018 (arising from CC/124/2017, District Forum North 24 Parganas) - Santi Ranjan Banik v. The New India Assurance Co. Ltd.

## Facts
The complainant, insured under a New India mediclaim policy, was admitted to a higher category of room than his entitlement. The insurer reimbursed room rent at the admissible rate and paid consultation, laboratory/diagnostic and other expenses, except medicines, in proportion. The District Forum rejected his complaint of short reimbursement, and he appealed arguing that consultation and diagnostic charges should have been paid in full under the policy clauses.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Clause 3.1(d): room rent, boarding and nursing limited to 1% of sum insured per day (2% for ICU/ICCU); if the room rate exceeds the limit, all other hospital expenses except medicines are paid in the same proportion as the admissible rate bears to the actual room rate.

## Decision
Dismissed (insurer's decision upheld). No specific amount quantified in the decision (or no amount awarded).

## Reasoning
Reading clause 3.1(d), the Commission held that once a patient is admitted to a room/ICU above the entitled category, all admissible expenses other than medicines are reimbursable at proportionate rates and not at actuals. It found no inaccuracy in the TPA's deductions and held that the complainant had misread the policy, so no relief was granted.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- New India Mediclaim Policy clause 3.1(d) - room rent cap and proportionate reimbursement (medicines excepted)

## Key quote
> "all other admissible expenses is reimbursable at proportionate rates and not on actual"
