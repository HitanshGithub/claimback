# CASE-001: Proportionate deduction - Allowed (Insurance Ombudsman, Mumbai, 2021)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** Insurance Ombudsman, Mumbai
- **Case reference:** Complaint Ref MUM-H-049-2021-1641 (Award No. IO/MUM/A/HI/ /2020-2021)
- **Decision date:** 2021-04-30
- **Category:** proportionate_deduction (Proportionate deduction)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - The New India Assurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** New India Mediclaim Policy (sum insured Rs 3,00,000 + Rs 20,000 cumulative bonus)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 2,77,712
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 21,000
- **Outcome:** Allowed (policyholder won)
- **Amount awarded:** not stated

## Facts
The complainant's wife was admitted to a Mumbai hospital in February 2020 with sepsis, cellulitis and uncontrolled diabetes, spent part of the stay in the ICU and died during the admission. The claim of Rs 2,77,712 was short-settled: the insurer disallowed ICU/room rent above eligibility and also reduced consultation, investigation and physiotherapy charges proportionately. The complainant accepted the room-rent and non-medical deductions but disputed the proportionate cuts (Rs 21,000 sought), producing the hospital tariff which showed that consultation, investigation and lab charges did not vary with room or ICU category.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
ICU charges of Rs 8,750/day exceeded the ICU limit of 2% of sum insured per day, so all other hospital expenses except medicines were paid in the same proportion under clause 3.2 (proportionate deduction); remaining deductions were non-medical items under Annexure 2.

## Decision
Allowed (policyholder won). No specific amount quantified in the decision (or no amount awarded).

## Reasoning
The Forum accepted the hospital tariff showing that all charges other than the bed charge were the same whether the patient was in a ward or the ICU. Disallowing room rent above the limit and non-medical items was in order, but reducing other charges proportionately when the hospital has no class-based tariff was held unjustified and against IRDAI guidelines; the insurer itself conceded that proportionate deduction would not apply to doctor fees and investigations at this hospital. The balance admissible expenses deducted proportionately were ordered to be paid (amount not quantified in the award).

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- New India Mediclaim Policy clause 3.1(b) - ICU/ICCU expenses up to 2% of sum insured per day
- Clause 3.2 - proportionate deduction (all other expenses except medicines)
- Annexure 2 - non-medical items
- IRDAI guidelines on proportionate deduction (as referred to by the Forum)

## Key quote
> "reducing other charges in proportion to the entitled room category in the absence of a class-based tariff is not justified and is against IRDAI guidelines in this regard"
