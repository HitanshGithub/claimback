# CASE-011: Reasonable and customary charges - Partly allowed (Insurance Ombudsman, Mumbai, 2021)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** Insurance Ombudsman, Mumbai
- **Case reference:** Complaint Ref MUM-H-049-2021-1787 (Award No. IO/MUM/A/HI/ /2020-2021)
- **Decision date:** 2021-04-30
- **Category:** reasonable_customary (Reasonable and customary charges)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - The New India Assurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** New India Mediclaim Policy (sum insured Rs 3,00,000)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 3,09,351
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 1,41,972
- **Outcome:** Partly allowed (policyholder partly won)
- **Amount awarded:** Rs 71,150

## Facts
The complainant's son, continuously insured since 2001, had surgery in Mumbai in August-September 2020 for a comminuted fracture of the upper right humerus after an accident. The claim of Rs 3,09,351 was settled for Rs 1,67,379. Deductions included Rs 81,500 out of a Rs 1,25,000 surgeon's fee under the reasonable & customary clause, excess room rent (Rs 3,500/day vs Rs 3,000 eligible) with proportionate deductions, C-arm, dressing, OT replacement medicines and non-medical items. It was the first claim in about 19 years of cover.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Surgeon's fee restricted under the Reasonable & Customary clause 2.37; excess room rent and proportionate deduction under clause 3.2; C-arm charges included in OT; dressing included in nursing; OT replacement medicines without break-up; non-medical items not payable.

## Decision
Partly allowed (policyholder partly won). Amount awarded: Rs 71,150.

## Reasoning
The Forum upheld the room-rent cap and proportionate deduction under clause 3.2 (which exempts only medicines) and the non-medical deductions. It allowed C-arm (Rs 16,000), dressing (Rs 3,500) and OT replacement (Rs 10,900) as genuinely billed. On the surgeon's fee, it held the insurer may apply the reasonable & customary clause by comparing prevailing charges in the area even without a specific cap, but to strike a balance allowed half (Rs 40,750) of the Rs 81,500 deducted.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- Clause 2.37 - reasonable and customary charges
- Clause 3.2 - room rent 1% of sum insured and proportionate deduction (except medicines)

## Key quote
> "even in the absence of a specific capping in the policy, the Company is within its right to limit the expenses payable for a particular procedure by comparing the charges prevalent in the same geographical area"
