# CASE-013: Reasonable and customary charges - Allowed (State Commission Gujarat, 2023)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** State Commission Gujarat (Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, Gujarat State, Ahmedabad)
- **Case reference:** First Appeal No. 690/2017 (arising from Complaint No. 426/2016, District Commission Vadodara) - Dr. Rikesh T. Majmudar v. United India Insurance Co. Ltd.
- **Decision date:** 2023-06-30
- **Category:** reasonable_customary (Reasonable and customary charges)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - United India Insurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** United India mediclaim policy (sum insured Rs 5,00,000)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 1,40,000
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 82,000
- **Outcome:** Allowed (policyholder won)
- **Amount awarded:** Rs 82,000

## Facts
The complainant, a surgeon, underwent cataract surgery on both eyes in February-March 2016 with foldable multifocal intraocular lenses, spending Rs 70,000 per eye (Rs 1,40,000 in total). The insurer paid Rs 29,000 per eye, allowing only Rs 10,000 per lens (the price of a unifocal lens) against Rs 51,000 billed for each multifocal lens. The District Commission awarded only Rs 20,000 of the Rs 82,000 deducted, and the complainant appealed.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
A multifocal lens primarily has a cosmetic benefit of eliminating spectacles; cost of spectacles, surgery for correction of vision and cosmetic treatment are excluded (clauses 4.6(b), 4.7), and only medically necessary, reasonable and customary expenses are payable (clauses 3.22, 3.33).

## Decision
Allowed (policyholder won). Amount awarded: Rs 82,000.

## Reasoning
The Commission held the procedure was a cataract surgery, not a cosmetic one, and the insurer produced no contrary evidence; the spectacles/contact lens exclusion concerns external aids, not an implanted lens. The policy set no limit on cataract charges or type of lens, so the insurer could not override the treating doctor's choice of implant or use the reasonable & customary clause to restrict it. The full Rs 82,000 was awarded with 8% interest from the date of complaint.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- Clause 3.22 - medical expenses
- Clause 3.33 - reasonable and customary charges
- Exclusion 4.6(b) - cosmetic treatment / correction of eyesight
- Exclusion 4.7 - cost of spectacles, contact lenses and hearing aids
- Narendra D. Jobanputra v. Oriental Insurance (Gujarat State Commission, Appeal No. 1378/2014)

## Key quote
> "Selection of type of implants cannot be decided by insurer."
