# CASE-004: Proportionate deduction - Partly allowed (State Commission Gujarat, 2023)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** State Commission Gujarat (Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, Gujarat State, Ahmedabad)
- **Case reference:** First Appeal No. 897/2022 (arising from Complaint No. 580/2019, District Commission Surat) - The Oriental Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Vijiyan Ponnu Nadar
- **Decision date:** 2023-05-10
- **Category:** proportionate_deduction (Proportionate deduction)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - The Oriental Insurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** Mediclaim policy, silver plan (sum insured Rs 3,00,000)
- **Amount claimed:** not stated
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 92,363
- **Outcome:** Partly allowed (policyholder partly won)
- **Amount awarded:** Rs 65,792

## Facts
The complainant was hospitalised in Surat for three days in October 2018 after slipping from a scooter. The insurer paid Rs 1,30,126 cashless and deducted Rs 92,363 under various heads, including proportionate deductions on surgeon fees, duty doctor, lab, X-ray, gases and physiotherapy, OT consumables and procedure charges treated as included in OT, non-medical items, nursing charges and a 10% co-payment on implants. The District Commission awarded the full Rs 92,363 and the insurer appealed.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Room rent eligibility was 1% of sum insured (Rs 3,000/day) and a higher room was taken, so proportionate deduction applied; the silver plan carries a 10% co-payment; nursing and non-medical items not payable; OT consumables and procedure charges included in OT charges.

## Decision
Partly allowed (policyholder partly won). Amount awarded: Rs 65,792.

## Reasoning
The State Commission found that the policy wording justified only four deductions: excess room rent (Rs 3,000), nursing charges (Rs 1,050), non-medical items (Rs 6,456) and the 10% co-payment on implants (Rs 16,065), totalling Rs 26,571. The insurer could not justify the proportionate cuts on surgeon/doctor fees, investigations, gases and physiotherapy or the 'included in OT' deductions (Rs 65,792), which amounted to deficiency in service. The District Commission's award was reduced from Rs 92,363 to Rs 65,792.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- Policy clause 1.1/1.2 - room rent 1% of sum insured per day and nursing charges
- Clause 3.8 - co-payment definition (10% silver plan)
- Clause 4.17 - non-medical expenses
- Industrial Promotion & Investment Corporation of Orissa v. New India Assurance (2016) 15 SCC 315 - cited by insurer

## Key quote
> "the appellant has shown deficiency in service"
