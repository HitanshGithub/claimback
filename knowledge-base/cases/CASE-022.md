# CASE-022: Pre-existing disease / non-disclosure - Dismissed (Supreme Court of India, 2009)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** Supreme Court of India (civil appeal from NCDRC)
- **Case reference:** Civil Appeal No. 2776 of 2002 - Satwant Kaur Sandhu v. New India Assurance Co. Ltd., (2009) 8 SCC 316
- **Decision date:** 2009-07-10
- **Category:** ped_non_disclosure (Pre-existing disease / non-disclosure)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - The New India Assurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** New India mediclaim policy (May 1990 - May 1991)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 23,217.80
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 23,217.80
- **Outcome:** Dismissed (insurer's decision upheld)
- **Amount awarded:** not stated

## Facts
The complainant's husband took a mediclaim policy in May 1990, answering 'sound health' and 'nil' to questions on health and treatment in the proposal. He fell ill in September 1990 and died in December 1990 at a nephrology hospital. The claim of Rs 23,217.80 was repudiated after the hospital certified he was a known diabetic for 16 years with chronic renal failure on regular haemodialysis. The District Forum allowed the complaint, but the State Commission and NCDRC ruled for the insurer.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Suppression of material facts about long-standing diabetes and chronic renal failure in the proposal form.

## Decision
Dismissed (insurer's decision upheld). No specific amount quantified in the decision (or no amount awarded).

## Reasoning
The Supreme Court held that when a proposal form asks specific questions, the proposer must truthfully disclose facts within his knowledge, and any fact that would influence a prudent insurer's decision is material. A person on regular haemodialysis could not have been unaware of his condition, so the 'sound health' answer was untrue to his knowledge and the insurer was justified in repudiating; the appeal was dismissed.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- Duty of disclosure - contract of utmost good faith (uberrimae fidei)
- Section 45 Insurance Act, 1938 (held not attracted)

## Key quote
> "There was clear suppression of material facts in regard to the health of the insured and, therefore, the respondent - insurer was fully justified in repudiating the insurance contract."
