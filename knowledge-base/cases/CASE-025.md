# CASE-025: Waiting period - Allowed (State Commission Delhi, 2026)

- **Forum:** State Commission Delhi (Delhi State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission)
- **Case reference:** First Appeal No. 579/2023 - S.B.I. General Insurance Co. Ltd. v. Surojit Pachal & Anr.
- **Decision date:** 2026-06-09
- **Category:** waiting_period (Waiting period)
- **Insurer type:** private general insurer - SBI General Insurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** SBI General mediclaim policy from July 2017 (described as a Rs 10 lakh top-up with Rs 3 lakh general cover)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 4,66,005
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 4,66,005
- **Outcome:** Allowed (policyholder won)
- **Amount awarded:** Rs 4,66,005

## Facts
The complainant took a mediclaim policy in July 2017 covering himself and his wife, who was also covered under a separate policy with another insurer. Between January and March 2018 she was hospitalised three times in Delhi for chronic renal failure and related conditions, and she died in hospital in March 2018. The other insurer paid part of the bills, but the respondent rejected reimbursement of the balance (Rs 4,66,005) and later also alleged concealment of pre-existing disease. The District Commission allowed the complaint in July 2023 and the insurer appealed.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Expenses related to chronic renal failure, hypertension and diabetes and their complications are excluded for the first year of cover; alleged concealment of pre-existing disease in the proposal form.

## Decision
Allowed (policyholder won). Amount awarded: Rs 4,66,005.

## Reasoning
The insurer never produced the proposal form, the policy terms or any pre-policy medical examination, and could not show that the terms and exclusions had been supplied to the insured; concealment was not proved. Relying on Manmohan Nanda v. United India Assurance and Bharat Watch Company v. National Insurance, the Commission held that an exclusion or waiting-period clause cannot be enforced if it was never communicated. The appeal was dismissed and the District Commission's award (Rs 4,66,005 with 6% interest, Rs 30,000 compensation, Rs 10,000 costs) stood.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- First-year exclusion for chronic renal failure, hypertension and diabetes (as pleaded by insurer)
- Manmohan Nanda v. United India Assurance (Civil Appeal No. 8386/2013, 06.12.2021)
- Bharat Watch Company v. National Insurance Co. Ltd. (Civil Appeal No. 3912/2019)
- IRDA (Protection of Policyholders' Interests) Regulations, 2002

## Key quote
> "the exclusion clause of the policy can only be resorted to if the insured has been duly supplied with the terms and conditions"
