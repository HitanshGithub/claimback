# CASE-002: Proportionate deduction - Partly allowed (Insurance Ombudsman, Mumbai, 2021)

> **Rules-era note:** decided before the IRDAI health insurance rules of 29 May 2024. The outcome may rest on rules that have since changed (for example the old 48-month pre-existing disease waiting period or the superseded 2020 standardization circular). Check the current rule in rules_regulatory.json before relying on it.

- **Forum:** Insurance Ombudsman, Mumbai
- **Case reference:** Complaint Ref MUM-H-049-2021-1770 (Award No. IO/MUM/A/HI/ /2021-2022)
- **Decision date:** 2021-04-30
- **Category:** proportionate_deduction (Proportionate deduction)
- **Insurer type:** public sector general insurer - The New India Assurance Co. Ltd.
- **Policy:** New India Floater Mediclaim Policy (sum insured Rs 3,00,000)
- **Amount claimed:** Rs 1,31,600
- **Amount in dispute:** Rs 74,265
- **Outcome:** Partly allowed (policyholder partly won)
- **Amount awarded:** not stated

## Facts
The insured was hospitalised in Mumbai for 7 days in April 2020 for acute febrile illness with chronic kidney disease and hypertension; cashless was not given. The reimbursement claim of Rs 1,31,600 was settled for Rs 57,335. The insured had occupied a room of Rs 4,500/day against an eligibility of Rs 3,000/day. The complainant disputed the deductions (Rs 74,265), mainly PPE kit and swab test charges, and later submitted additional swab test bills.

## Insurer's reason for deduction / rejection
Excess room and nursing charges disallowed (eligible Rs 3,000/day); doctor fees and hospital service charges paid in proportion to the eligible room rent under policy condition 3.1; diet charges not payable; PPE kits paid for only one day for a non-Covid patient; investigation charges lacked a break-up. On reassessment the TPA found a further Rs 6,538 payable.

## Decision
Partly allowed (policyholder partly won). No specific amount quantified in the decision (or no amount awarded).

## Reasoning
The Forum held that deducting diet charges, excess room charges and the proportionate reduction of other charges were as per policy terms and in order. However, disallowing PPE kit charges during Covid times was not justified even for a non-Covid patient, and the additional investigation bills had to be considered. The insurer was directed to pay the PPE deduction, the admissible investigation charges and the Rs 6,538 it had already found payable.

## Rules, clauses and precedents cited
- New India Floater Mediclaim policy condition 3.1 - room rent limit and proportionate deduction
- Non-payable diet charges

## Key quote
> "the disallowance of PPE Kit charges during Covid times even though the complainant was a non-covid patient, is not justified"
